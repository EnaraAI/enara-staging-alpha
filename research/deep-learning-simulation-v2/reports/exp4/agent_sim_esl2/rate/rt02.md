STUDENT: Hello! Can you give me an exercise on a, an and the?
TUTOR: Here is your exercise. Write the missing word.

Write a or an: She has ___ orange in her bag. (answer with only the missing word)
STUDENT: an
TUTOR: Correct: "an".

Now one on your own, with a, an or the.

Write a, an or the: It is cold. Please close ___ door. (answer with only the missing word)
STUDENT: the
TUTOR: Correct: "the". We both know which door.

Now a freer task. Write two short sentences of your own. Use a or an in the first, and the in the second, about the same thing.
STUDENT: I have a cat. The cat is black.
TUTOR: Both sentences are correct: "a cat" names it the first time, and "The cat" talks about the same cat again.

Recap: use a or an the first time you name one thing. Use the when we both know which thing, like "the door" in a room.

Tell me the rule in your own words.
STUDENT: I think: a singular countable noun needs a/an (or the).
TUTOR: Yes, a singular countable noun needs a word before it. But the choice is not free: a or an names a thing for the first time; the is for a thing we both already know.

(next day)
STUDENT: Hello again! Can we practise a, an and the again today?
TUTOR: Happy to. Let's go past the basics and focus on the choice that causes most slips: a/an versus the.
A quick reminder: a or an introduces something new to the reader; the points to something both of you can identify, because it was mentioned or is unique in the situation. And a or an follows the sound, not the letter.
Example of my own: A doctor came in with an umbrella. The doctor put the umbrella by the door. "The door" works even on first mention, because the room has only one obvious door.
Your task: write a short text of three sentences about an object (an orange, a book, an elephant...). Introduce it, then refer back to it, and include one "the" that is used because the thing is unique in context. Then explain each article choice in a few words.
STUDENT: I bought an orange. The orange was sweet. The shop was busy.
TUTOR: All three articles work: "an orange" introduces it (vowel sound), "The orange" refers back to it, and "The shop" is identifiable because buying implies one particular shop.
Let's check the basics on a fresh item. Write a or an: She has ___ orange in her bag. (answer with only the missing word)
STUDENT: an
TUTOR: "an" is correct: She has an orange in her bag.
One more without any help. Write a or an: He wants ___ umbrella. (answer with only the missing word)
STUDENT: an
TUTOR: "an" is correct: He wants an umbrella.
A harder variant: Write a or an: It was ___ hour late, and she used ___ unusual map. (answer with only the two words)
STUDENT: an, an
TUTOR: "an, an" is correct: hour starts with a vowel sound because the h is silent, and unusual starts with a vowel sound too.
To pull today together:
A or an introduces something new, and the choice follows the first sound, not the first letter, as in your "I bought an orange."
The points to something both of you can identify, mentioned before ("The orange was sweet.") or clear from the situation ("The shop was busy.").

TUTOR: Yes, let's practise! There are three ways to suggest something, and each one needs a different verb form. That's why they can be confusing. We'll take them one at a time.

**Step 1: How about + verb-ing**

After "How about", use the **-ing** form of the verb.

- How about + have → How about **having** a picnic?
- How about + watch → How about **watching** a film tonight?

Here's my thinking for the first one: I want to suggest "have a picnic". I start with "How about", then I change "have" to "having". It's a friendly question, not an order, so the other person can say yes or no.

**Your turn:** This suggestion has a mistake. Write it correctly:

"How about go to the park?"

TUTOR: Yes, that's right: "How about going to the park?" is correct.

About "to go": "How about to go to the park?" is not correct English. After "How about", we use the **-ing** form, not "to + verb".

A simple way to remember it: "about" works like other small words such as *for*, *of* or *without*. After these words, a verb usually takes the -ing form:

- I'm thinking **about moving**.
- Thanks **for helping**.

So "How about" follows the same pattern.

=========== CASE r1-S049-GR-04-A@4 ===========
Lesson: Life and time — Contrast present perfect and past simple.

CONVERSATION SO FAR
STUDENT: Hi, teacher. Can you help me practise? I want to be better with present perfect. I think I understand it, but I am not sure.
TUTOR: Yes, let's practise. We'll go one step at a time.

**Step 1: Is the time finished?**

The key question is whether the time is finished or still continuing.

- **Present perfect** (*have/has + past participle*) connects the past to **now**. We often use it with **for** and **since**.

Here is my example, step by step:

*"I bought my phone last month."*
1. Find the time words: *last month*.
2. Is that time finished? Yes.
3. So we use past simple: *bought*, not *have bought*.

Compare it with the course example: *"I have lived here for five years."* I still live here, so the time is not finished.

**Your turn:** This sentence has a mistake.
*"I have visited my sister last weekend."*

Write the correct sentence, and tell me which time words helped you decide.
STUDENT: I think it is "I visited my sister last weekend" because last weekend is finished. But I am not sure, teacher. Is "have visited" wrong always?
TUTOR: Yes, that's correct: **"I visited my sister last weekend."** Your reason is also right. *Last weekend* is finished, so we use past simple.

**Is "have visited" always wrong?** No. It is only wrong here because of the finished time words.

"Have visited" works well when there is **no finished time**, for example when you talk about experience in your life:

- *"I have visited Rome three times."* (in my life, up to now)

But if you add a finished time like *last weekend*, *in 2019* or *two days ago*, we usually switch to past simple:

- *"I visited Rome in 2019."*

So the time words decide.

Now your question: **"I have bought this jacket yesterday."** This sentence is not okay.

Look at the time word at the end: *yesterday*. Ask the same question you used for *last weekend*: is that time finished, or does it continue until now?

STUDENT: I think "I bought this jacket yesterday" is correct. Yesterday is finished, so we use past simple. But I am not sure, teacher. Is "have bought" never correct with yesterday?
TUTOR: Correct: **"I bought this jacket yesterday."** Your reason is right too. *Yesterday* is finished, so past simple fits.

**Is "have bought" never correct with yesterday?**
In standard English, we don't use present perfect with *yesterday*, or with other finished time words like *last week*, *in 2020* or *two days ago*.

"Have bought" is fine when there is no finished time, for example:
- *"I have bought a new jacket."* (the result matters now: I have it)

If you want to say *when*, switch to past simple.

About your question: no, "We have moved to this street in 2018" is not correct, even if you still live there. A finished time like "in 2018" needs past simple. To talk about how long you have lived there, we use "for" or "since".

- Finished time (yesterday, last weekend, in 2018): past simple.

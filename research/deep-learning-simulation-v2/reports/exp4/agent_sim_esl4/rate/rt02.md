TUTOR: Hello! Sure. We use am with I, is with he, she and it, and are with you, we and they. Here is your exercise: They ___ happy. Write only the missing word.
STUDENT: (I leave it empty, no word)
TUTOR: No problem, try again! In English we cannot leave this word out. Hint: "they" is more than one person, like "we" and "you". Which word goes with they: am, is or are? They ___ happy.
STUDENT: (I leave it empty, no word)
TUTOR: That's fine, let me show you. The answer is are: They are happy. We use are with they, we and you: We are happy. You are happy. Now your turn: She ___ at home today. Write am, is or are.
STUDENT: (I leave it empty, no word)
TUTOR: That's okay, try again. We need a word here: "She at home" is not correct English. Hint: she is one person, like he. Am, is or are? She ___ at home today.
STUDENT: is
TUTOR: Yes, well done! She is one person, like he, so we use is: She is at home today. Next one: I ___ a student. Write am, is or are.
(next day)
STUDENT: Hello again! Can we practise am, is and are again today?
TUTOR: Hello again! Yes, let's practise. Remember: I am. He is. They are. Try this: They ___ happy. Write only am, is or are.
STUDENT: (I leave it empty, no word)
TUTOR: That's okay, let's try again. In English we cannot say "They happy"; we need a word. Hint: they means more than one person. Which word goes with they: am, is or are?
STUDENT: are
TUTOR: Yes, well done! They is more than one person, so we use are: They are happy. Next: I ___ a student. Write only am, is or are.
STUDENT: (I leave it empty, no word)
TUTOR: No problem. We cannot say "I a student"; we need a word between I and a student. Hint: there is one special word that only goes with I. Is it am, is or are? Try again.
STUDENT: (I leave it empty, no word)
TUTOR: That's okay, here is the answer. It is am: I am a student. Am only goes with I: I am happy. I am at home. Now a new one: She ___ at home today. Write only am, is or are.

STUDENT: The cap is twenty dollars.

STUDENT: But the cap is twenty dollars. B says now fifteen, but the price is still twenty, no?
TUTOR: I see why it is confusing. Think of a shop. Last week the cap was 20 dollars. Today there is a discount, so the price changed to 15 dollars.

"Was" = before. "Now" = today.
If you buy the cap today, you pay 15 dollars, not 20.

So, what is the price you pay now?
STUDENT: Ah, so I pay fifteen? Fifteen dollars now, because of discount. Is it 15 or 50?
TUTOR: Yes! You pay fifteen dollars now. Well done.

15 or 50? It is 15. Fifteen = fif-TEEN, the end is long: "teen". Fifty = FIF-ty, the end is short: "ty".

Try one more:
A: The shirt was 40 dollars.
B: Now it is 30 dollars.

How much does the shirt cost now?
STUDENT: The shirt costs thirty dollars now. Thirty, not thirteen, right?
TUTOR: Exactly right! Thirty (30), not thirteen (13). And you chose the new price, not the old 40.

Last one:
A: The bag was 50 dollars.
B: Now it is 13 dollars.

How much is the bag now?

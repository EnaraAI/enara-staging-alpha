# ESL7 new learning rules: six course outcomes (SIMULATION ONLY, DRAFT)

Additions to `config/agent_sim_esl6/LEARNING_RULES.md` section 1b for the six A1-A2 **course** outcomes of `data/outcomes/outcomes.json`. Same use as section 1b: W1 is the primary wrong belief, Ck is the correct counterpart of Wk, no W2 means no C2, starting strengths by the section-1 bands. Sections 2-7 of the ESL6 rules apply unchanged; section 4a applies with these rows (an item tests Ck only if Wk could change its answer; see `ITEM_BELIEF_MAP_NEW.json`).

New error code (not in framework section 9; a reading strategy, not an Arabic-L1 transfer error, so it is a hypothesis about A1-A2 readers in general): `keyword_matching` - the learner answers from the first part of the text that matches the type of answer asked for (name, number, time, day, place; for a main-idea question, the first sentence) without checking the other words of the question. All other codes are reused from framework section 9 / `docs/LEARNING_OUTCOMES.md` PART 2. `request_directness` is used here, as asked in the build brief, also for an apology with no reason (a bare "Sorry."), which widens its PART 2 definition.

| # | Outcome | Correct belief (C) | Wrong belief(s): error type — belief — Arabic-L1 learner example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 34 | EN-C-A1-RD-very-short-texts | the answer is the name, number, time or place in the text that fits all the words of the question | W1 `keyword_matching` — the first name/number/time/place in the text of the asked type is the answer — notice "Mr Sami is here in the morning. Mrs Hana is here in the afternoon."; Q "Who is here in the afternoon?" -> "Mr Sami" |
| 35 | EN-C-A2-RD-short-everyday-texts | the main idea is what the whole text is for, and a detail answer fits all the words of the question | W1 `keyword_matching` — the first sentence is the main idea, and the first detail of the asked type is the answer — email starting "I hope you are well." -> main idea "to ask about the family"; Q "until what time?" -> the first time in the text |
| 36 | EN-C-A1-WR-simple-sentences | each idea is its own sentence with a capital and a full stop, and each present sentence has am/is/are (or have/has) | W1 `run_on_and` — a text is one long line joined with "and", no capitals or full stops — "my sister is ten and she has a cat and she is nice" ; W2 `copula_omission` — no am/is/are is needed in a present sentence — "My sister ten. She nice." |
| 37 | EN-C-A2-WR-linked-short-text | clauses are linked with and, but or because chosen by meaning, inside full sentences, and past events take past verb forms | W1 `run_on_and` — clauses are chained with "and" whatever the meaning, with no full stops — "yesterday it rained and i stayed home and i was sad and my friend came" ; W2 `tense_aspect` — a time word shows the past, so verbs stay in the present — "Yesterday it rains. I stay at home because I am ill." |
| 38 | EN-C-A1-FN-basic-interaction | a personal question is answered (or asked) with a full simple sentence: subject + verb, with am/is/are where needed | W1 `fragment_answer` (new code) — one word or a fragment is a full answer, as in a short spoken reply — "How old are you?" -> "Ten." ; W2 `copula_omission` — no am/is/are is needed — "I ten years old." / "Where you from?" |
| 39 | EN-C-A2-FN-routine-exchanges | requests, apologies, suggestions and questions use a polite frame (Can/Could I ... please?, I'm sorry + reason, How about ...?, Excuse me, when does ...?) with question word order | W1 `request_directness` — a bare order, "I want", a bare "Sorry" or an imperative is a normal request, apology or suggestion, as in Arabic aʿtini / ana ʿayiz — "Give me your ruler." / "Sorry, teacher." / "Come Tuesday at four." ; W2 `verb_subject_order` — a question keeps statement order after the question word — "When the next train leaves?" |

`fragment_answer` is also new (not in framework section 9); it is a hypothesis for A1 written chat, typical of beginners who transfer short spoken answers. W2 was added only where the error is a listed Arabic-L1 type of that outcome (`copula_omission`, `tense_aspect`, `verb_subject_order`); the two reading outcomes have one wrong belief.

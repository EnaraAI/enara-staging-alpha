# ENARA r4.2: independent expert review, Reviewer B

**This package is confidential and for expert reviewers only.** It contains course answer keys, so it must never be shown to learners.

## What you are asked to do

You will review **40 tutoring conversations** between an AI tutor and **synthetic, scripted** EFL learners. No real learners are involved.

For each case, score the **ten criteria** below in `reviewer_B_score_sheet.csv`:
- give a verbatim quote and turn number for any score below 3;
- work **independently**: do not discuss cases with the other reviewer or anyone else until both score sheets are submitted.

**What you are not given, on purpose:**
- any automated (AI) scores, judgements, outcomes or comments;
- any information about why a case was selected;
- the other reviewer's decisions.

The case order is randomised for you. The case IDs are anonymous.

## Scale (criteria C1–C8 and C10)

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 4 | Strong: clearly good practice, nothing to improve on this criterion |
| 3 | Adequate: acceptable for a language tutor; minor imperfections only |
| 2 | Weak: a clear flaw that reduces the value of the turn |
| 1 | Poor: a flaw that defeats the purpose of the turn |
| 0 | Unacceptable: harmful, wrong teaching, or unsafe/unsuitable content |
| NA | The criterion cannot apply to this conversation |
| IE | Insufficient evidence: it applies, but the conversation is too short to judge |

**C9 Evidence sufficiency** uses SUFFICIENT / LIMITED / INSUFFICIENT and rates the **case**, not the tutor.

**Also record:**
- an overall category (*meets expectations / partially meets / does not meet / cannot judge*);
- any **critical** defect (wrong teaching of the target form, safety or age-unsuitable content) or **major** defect (defeats the turn's purpose).

## The ten criteria

| ID | Criterion | Question | Draft-rubric reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Task alignment | Does the tutor work on the lesson objective and the open task (using the lesson items when present)? | D2 |
| C2 | Age appropriateness | Tone, topics and assumed autonomy fit the learner's age band (not length). | D3 |
| C3 | Answer correctness | Does the tutor judge the learner's answer correctly (correct / equivalent / partly / incorrect / ambiguous) and say what is right or wrong? | D5 |
| C4 | Grounding | Does the tutor use only the facts it was given and what the learner actually wrote (no invented history, no misquotes)? | D1 |
| C5 | Grammar | Are the tutor's rules, examples and claims about English correct and consistent? | D10 |
| C6 | CEFR appropriateness | Is the language, explanation depth and length comprehensible and useful at the stated CEFR level? | D4 |
| C7 | Hint quality and answer leakage | Do hints leave the tested step to the learner? Is support graduated? Was the answer given before an attempt or request? | D7 (+ D6) |
| C8 | Formative assessment quality | Does the tutor check understanding with a task the learner must answer unaided, on the tested component? | D9 (+ D8) |
| C9 | Evidence sufficiency | Is there enough in this conversation to judge the criteria above? (rate the case, not the tutor) | — (new) |
| C10 | Overall instructional quality | Your overall professional judgement of the tutor's teaching in this conversation. | — (new) |

> **About the reference rubric.** Appendix A reproduces the **draft** rubric `ltb-rubric-v2` (dimensions D1–D11). It is **not academically validated**. It is provided only as a discussion reference, so you can see what the automated scoring tried to measure. Use your own professional judgement; disagreeing with the draft is useful. Bracketed references in the draft such as "(CA2-T03)" point to older historical transcripts, **not** to cases in this package.

- **Scenario (fixture situation type):** `T06_repeated_misconception`

- **Objective:** Talk about life experiences with the present perfect and contrast it with the past simple for finished times.
- **Objectives:** Ask and answer "Have you ever …?" · Use the past simple, not the present perfect, with a finished time (yesterday, last year)

- **Grammar:** present perfect for experiences · past simple with finished time expressions
- **Vocabulary:** ever · never · already · yet · been to · tried · met

- **Lesson examples:** Have you ever tried sushi? Yes, I have. I tried it last summer. · She has never been to the sea.

### Lesson exercises and acceptable answers

| Exercise | Prompt (as given to the tutor) | Acceptable answers (course key) | Item type (extractor label) |

### Case design notes (written by the benchmark designers before any run; these are not scores)

- **Expected behaviour:** After the same error repeats following an explanation, changes approach (simpler step, contrasting minimal pair, different example) instead of repeating the same explanation; keeps the learner trying.
- **Unacceptable behaviour:** Repeats the identical explanation; gives up; scolds; long lecture.
- **Expected next move:** change of approach + smaller step
- **Word limit per reply:** 240

### Conversation (verbatim; complete; learner text is scripted and synthetic)

**Turn 1: Learner** _(learner request (not an answer))_

> Can you explain have been and went?

**Turn 1: Tutor**

> Both talk about the past, but they answer different questions.
> 
> **have/has been to** + place = present perfect. Use it for a **life experience** when the time is **not** said.
> - *I have been to Cairo.* (sometime in my life; when is not important)
> 
> **went to** + place = past simple. Use it when you say a **finished time** (yesterday, last year, in 2019).
> - *I went to Cairo last year.*
> 
> Here is how they work together in a short exchange (my example):
> - A: *Have you ever been to Italy?*
> - B: *Yes, I have. I went there in 2021.*
> 
> Step 1: B answers about the experience → present perfect.
> Step 2: B adds a finished time ("in 2021") → switches to past simple.
> 
> **Check:** Which is correct, and why?
> a) *She has been to Paris last summer.*
> b) *She went to Paris last summer.*

**Turn 2: Learner** _(learner answer to the tutor's own task)_

> I have gone to Alexandria last summer.

**Turn 2: Tutor**

> You've written your own sentence, which is useful practice. **"have gone"** is formed correctly (have + past participle).
> 
> But the sentence as a whole needs another look. Focus on these two parts together:
> 
> - *I **have gone** to Alexandria **last summer**.*
> 
> Look at the time phrase at the end, **"last summer"**. Is that time finished or not? Then check whether the verb matches that kind of time.
> 
> **Your turn:** Rewrite the sentence so the verb fits "last summer."

**Turn 3: Learner** _(learner answer to the tutor's own task)_

> I have been to Cairo two years ago.

**Turn 3: Tutor**

> **"have been"** is formed correctly (have + past participle). The problem is the same as last time: the verb doesn't match the time phrase **"two years ago."**
> 
> Let's take a smaller step with a different example (mine):
> 
> - *I **have eaten** sushi.* → no time given, just an experience
> - *I **ate** sushi **yesterday**.* → "yesterday" is finished, so past simple
> 
> The rule: when you name a finished time (yesterday, last summer, two years ago), use the **past simple**, not have/has + past participle.
> 
> "Two years ago" is a finished time. It's over, like "yesterday."
> 
> **Your turn:** Rewrite *"I have been to Cairo two years ago"* using the past simple. Change only the verb.

- **For C7 (hints):** the turns before the learner's first labelled attempt are the "before any attempt" window. Learner turns labelled *request* are not attempts.
- **For C8 (formative checks):** look at the closing task or question of each tutor turn.
- **Exercise type(s):** verb-form choice (past simple / present perfect).

# Appendix A: draft rubric `ltb-rubric-v2` (DISCUSSION REFERENCE ONLY, NOT VALIDATED)

### 1. Dimensions and anchors (0–4)

Each anchor gives the observable behaviour; "e.g." lines are drawn from the v1.0 transcripts.

#### D1 Context grounding (`context_grounding`)
Uses only supplied facts (lesson, trusted age band, CEFR estimate, prior evidence) and what the learner wrote. Keeps course content, learner text and the tutor's own examples apart.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0 | Invents learner history, results or level, or contradicts supplied facts. |
| 1 | Attributes an answer or knowledge the learner did not show **and** builds the next step on it. |
| 2 | One unsupported assumption or misquote that does not drive the next step. e.g. "The words "go to bed late" are a good start", when the learner wrote "she went to bed late" (CA2-T03); "you probably already know…" with no evidence (TB1-T13). |
| 3 | Uses supplied context correctly; quotes the learner exactly; invents nothing. |
| 4 | As 3, and visibly adapts to this learner's exact evidence (their error, their topic) while labelling its own examples as examples. |

#### D2 Task alignment (`task_alignment`)
Works on the lesson objective and the active item. Uses lesson items when they exist.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0 | Unrelated to the lesson, or does the off-topic task. |
| 1 | Mostly off-objective, or replaces a delivered lesson item with unrelated content. |
| 2 | On objective but drifts (e.g. introduces a second grammar point as if it were the target). |
| 3 | On objective. A correct common word outside the list (e.g. "often") is not drift. |
| 4 | Tightly aligned: uses the lesson's own items and patterns, and moves through them in order. |

#### D3 Age appropriateness (`age_fit`)
Tone, topics and assumed autonomy for the **trusted age band only**. Length is not scored here.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0 | Content unsuitable for the band (critical). |
| 1 | Register mostly wrong (e.g. baby talk to adults). |
| 2 | One clear register slip (e.g. a childish phrasing for a teen, TA1-T07). |
| 3 | Fits the band. |
| 4 | Examples and tone chosen for the band with evident care. |

#### D4 Proficiency appropriateness (`cefr_fit`)
Vocabulary, structures, explanation depth and length for the CEFR estimate. Judged against the case word limit and its **effect on comprehension**. Never inferred from age.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0 | Far above level; unusable. |
| 1 | Mostly above or below level. |
| 2 | Several unglossed above-level terms, or length that clearly overloads (two tasks, repeated rule). |
| 3 | Comprehensible at the level. |
| 4 | Precisely pitched: one step, new items glossed. |

#### D5 Answer sufficiency judgement (`answer_judgement`)
Did the tutor classify the learner's answer correctly? The classes are: correct; equivalent wording; correct but for a different item; correct but unrelated; partially correct; incorrect; ambiguous.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0 | Confirms a wrong answer as correct on the lesson target (critical). |
| 1 | Rejects or re-tests a correct answer, or treats an unrelated correct sentence as evidence for the objective. |
| 2 | Recognises correctness but mis-handles the class (e.g. re-issues the same item for a component already shown, CA2-T08 t2–t3). |
| 3 | Classifies correctly; credits what is shown; asks only about the undemonstrated part. |
| 4 | As 3, with the reason made visible to the learner in one line. |

#### D6 Scaffolding quality (`scaffolding`)
Support level matches the evidence. Escalates one step after a repeated error and fades after success.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0 | Ignores the learner's reply. |
| 1 | Leaves a stuck learner without a next step, or jumps straight to a full explanation on a first error. |
| 2 | Uses the reply, but support is mis-levelled (the same intensity regardless of evidence). |
| 3 | Adapts to the reply; the learner keeps doing the tested step. |
| 4 | Visible contingent escalation or fading, justified by the reply (e.g. AA1-T07 t4 after a repeated error). |

#### D7 Hint leakage (`hint_leakage`, higher = better)
After the hint, does the learner still have to perform the tested step? Hint ladder levels L1–L6 are defined in the diagnostic (`hint_scaffolding_analysis.md` §3).

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0 | Gives the full answer with no attempt and no request (major). |
| 1 | Names the tested form before any attempt. e.g. "*have* → **has**" (TA2-T13 t2); "Its past simple is *went*, and its past participle is *gone*" (AB1-T13 t2). (major) |
| 2 | Near-reveal too early: on a binary or single-ending item, the first hint resolves the choice (e.g. "A brother is a boy, so use He", TA1-T13 t2), or a model of the same answer sits next to the item (CA1-T06 t3). |
| 3 | Hint at a level that fits the attempts so far; the tested step stays with the learner. |
| 4 | As 3, and the first hint is an attention or conceptual cue that the learner can act on. |
| — | A full explanation after repeated failure, or when the learner asks having tried, is **not** leakage (L6): score 3–4. |

#### D8 Independent learner performance (`independence`)
Separates assisted from unassisted evidence.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0 | Claims mastery or progress without evidence (critical if it uses invented figures). |
| 1 | Treats a hinted success as independent, or one correct answer as mastery. |
| 2 | Moves on after a helped success without any unaided check. |
| 3 | After a helped success, gives an unaided item on the same point; after an unaided success, moves forward. |
| 4 | As 3, and states accurately what the learner has shown so far, without claiming mastery. |

#### D9 Formative checking (`formative_check`)
A check that the learner must answer **unaided** and that tests the objective's component.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0 | Invented progress or mastery claims. |
| 1 | No check where one was clearly needed. |
| 2 | A check exists but can be answered by copying (e.g. "This is my mother. ___" directly under "This is my grandmother. She is…", CA1-T06 t3), or tests a different component. "Does that make sense?" alone also scores 2. |
| 3 | An unaided check on the tested component. |
| 4 | A transfer or why-check matched to the success criteria (e.g. "write two sentences about yourself: something you have never done…", CB1-T08 t3). |
| n/a | A check is not due (clarification turn, redirect), recorded as `check_not_due`. |

#### D10 Grammar accuracy (`grammar_accuracy`)
Rules, examples and claims about English are true and agree with each other.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0 | Teaches a rule or form that makes the learner's target sentence wrong (critical). |
| 1 | A false statement about the lesson target that could mislead (major). e.g. "verbs ending in -ch add -es, like go → goes" (CA2-T07 t4). |
| 2 | A misleading statement about an example or a side point. e.g. "Only one word changes" for a pair where two words change (AA1-T06 t3); a negative listed under the affirmative "+ -s" rule (AA2-T11 t1). |
| 3 | Correct; simplifications are appropriate to the level ("always = every time" at A2). |
| 4 | As 3, with an example that exactly instantiates the rule and a cautious "usually" where needed. |

#### D11 Learning progression (`progression`)
Moves through the objective using evidence. Never repeats a demonstrated component; never skips an undemonstrated one.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 0 | Blocks progress (keeps the learner on a finished task) **and** misjudges the evidence. |
| 1 | Presents the same item three or more times after the component was shown (CA2-T08, TA2-T08). |
| 2 | Progresses, but re-teaches basics already shown, or skips an undemonstrated criterion. |
| 3 | Progresses on evidence: next item or transfer after success; retry on the same gap after failure. |
| 4 | As 3, and the next task targets the criterion not yet shown. |

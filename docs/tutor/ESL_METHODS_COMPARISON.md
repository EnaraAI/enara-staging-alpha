# ESL teaching standards compared with our 23 methods

19 Nov 2026. Tutor track. Research note, no code changed.

The repo's research summary for Arabic speakers (`docs/ENARA_ESL_FRAMEWORK.md` on the Track 2 branch) already covers Nation's four strands, explicit grammar, corrective feedback, task-based teaching and the CEFR. This note builds on it and checks the tutor against the certificates and standards that ESL teaching is judged by.

## 1. What counts as "certified" in ESL

| Source | What it is | What it says about teaching |
|---|---|---|
| CELTA / DELTA (Cambridge) | The main teacher certificates | No single required method. Trainees use lesson shapes: present, practise, produce; test, teach, test; task-based; engage, study, activate. |
| TKT and TKT: Young Learners (Cambridge) | Teacher knowledge tests | Covers those shapes plus guided discovery and Total Physical Response (TPR, responding to spoken commands with actions). The young-learners part adds scaffolding and practice activities: memory games, true/false, songs, TPR, stories, listen-and-do. |
| CELT-P (Cambridge) | Certificate for teachers of 6–12 year olds, 120 hours | Built around listening and speaking for children. Current status unclear; to confirm. |
| CEFR + Companion Volume 2020 (Council of Europe) | Level standard, Pre-A1 to C2 | "Action-oriented": learners use the language to do real things. Adds mediation, Pre-A1, and descriptors for ages 7–10 and 11–15. |
| TESOL 6 Principles | Practice standard | Know your learners; create conditions for learning (safe to make mistakes); design from clear objectives; adapt as you teach; track and assess progress; work with other teachers. |
| Research consensus | — | Nation's four strands (input, output, language focus, fluency); focus on form; corrective feedback that prompts self-repair works better than recasting the correct form alone (Lyster & Ranta). |

## 2. Compared with our 23 methods

### Covered well

| ESL practice | Our method or switch |
|---|---|
| Present, practise, produce | Direct Instruction (1), Worked Examples (12), guided practice, own-sentence task |
| Feedback that prompts self-repair | Formative Assessment (20) + Track 2 `feedbackLadder` |
| Focus on the learner's own error | `liveError` |
| Spaced review and recall | Retrieval Practice (9), Spaced Repetition (10), Interleaving (11) |
| Know your learners, adapt | Differentiated (18), Adaptive Pathways (19), learner profile, age band |
| Safe to make mistakes | Kids band rules: praise effort, no "wrong!" |
| CEFR levels, can-do outcomes | A1–A2 outcome bank |

### Present but not working for language learners yet

| ESL practice | Nearest method | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Guided discovery (B1+) | Socratic (2), Inquiry (5) | Never fired; all tested learners were beginners |
| Scaffolding for young learners | Scaffolded Instruction (3) | Never fired in Track 2 |
| Stories for young learners | Narrative-Based (22) | Only a framing line, not real stories |
| Games for young learners | Gamification (21) | Only a framing line |

### Missing

| ESL practice | Why it matters | Can the tutor do it? |
|---|---|---|
| Task-based / communicative practice | Core of CELTA and the CEFR | Yes: avatar role-plays (order food, job interview), then correction |
| Test, teach, test | Skips what adults already know | Yes: quick check first, teach only the gaps |
| Fluency practice | One of Nation's four strands | Yes: timed repeats of known language |
| Understandable input | Lots of reading and listening at level | Yes: short graded texts and avatar audio |
| Lexical chunks | Fixed phrases ("Can I have…") | Yes: add to vocabulary lessons |
| Kids: TPR / listen-and-do | Core of the young-learners syllabus | Partly: tap or drag pictures after avatar commands |
| Kids: songs and chants | Core of the young-learners syllabus | Yes: avatar chants |
| Kids: Pre-A1 | Level for very young learners | Yes: add a Pre-A1 outcome set |
| Mediation (CEFR) | Explaining meaning, also via Arabic | Later, B1+ |

### Less important for ESL beginners

Case-Based (7), Concept Mapping (17), Bloom's (23), Think-Aloud (16): fine for older and advanced learners, but no ESL certificate puts them first. Analogical Reasoning (14) is useful for one thing: contrasting English with Arabic.

## 3. What this means for the tutor

1. **Four named lesson shapes**, picked by the planner:
   - Present, practise, produce: beginners and kids.
   - Test, teach, test: adults who may already know part of it.
   - Task-based: B1+ and adults with a real-life goal.
   - Listen-and-do with a story or chant: kids.
2. **Add the missing strands:** fluency practice and understandable input.
3. **Kids pack:** TPR-style tap tasks, chants, real short stories, Pre-A1 outcomes, scaffolding on.
4. **Keep** the rule shown first for beginners, the prompt-first feedback ladder and spaced review.

## 4. What ESL6 adds

ESL6 (`reports/exp4/AGENT_SIM_ESL6.md`, branch `claude/tutor-esl6`) found the ESL5 setting level with the plain baseline on a 7-day delayed grammar test. Grammar switches alone did not help, which supports widening the tutor beyond grammar study rather than tuning it further.

## 5. Caveats

- The CELTA syllabus came mostly from secondary copies, not the current official version.
- CELT-P status is unclear: one provider says it is discontinued; Cambridge pages still list it.
- TESOL wording comes from TESOL's own summary table.
- Check against cambridgeenglish.org and tesol.org before citing formally.

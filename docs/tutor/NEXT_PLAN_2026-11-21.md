## Step 0: under-9 clash (switch `childRule`)
**Problem:** for under-9s, `childShort` allows 3 sentences of at most 12 words plus one task. The rule block asks for a rule, a right example and a wrong example plus a task, and the length trim cuts parts of it. Under-9s scored 68 vs 72 for BASE (ESL7b).

**Fix:** for under-9s, the rule block becomes a child version that fits the limit:
1. one rule sentence of at most 10 words, with no grammar words;
2. one right example of at most 8 words, and **no wrong example** (a wrong sentence confuses young children and doesn't fit);
3. the task.

The `ruleOnWrong` line gets the same child form (one sentence and the task). `trimChildReply` keeps the rule sentence, the example and the task, in that order.

**Tests:** an under-9 reply fits 3 × 12 words plus the task; the rule survives the trim; nothing changes for 9 and over.

## 1. `kidsAudioVisual` (under 9)
**What it does:**
- Each word and short sentence from the kids pack (Cambridge Pre A1 Starters list, 495 words, already in `data/kids/prea1`) can be played aloud and shown with a picture.
- The child answers by tapping one of 2–4 pictures or choices, or by speaking (see below).

**Pictures:**
- An openly licensed set with commercial use allowed: **OpenMoji (CC BY-SA 4.0)**, with **Twemoji graphics (CC BY 4.0)** as a fallback, and attribution shown on an About page.
- Not Cambridge's own picture book (copyrighted). Not ARASAAC (non-commercial licence).
- Each of the 495 words is mapped to a picture. Words with no good picture stay text-only. I'll report the coverage.
- Pictures are stored as static files, with no runtime calls.

**Voice (text-to-speech):**
- **Azure Neural TTS**, using the same Azure Speech resource already used for pronunciation.
- The word list and item sentences are generated **once**, ahead of time, and stored as audio files. That costs about a few dollars in total, gives no runtime latency and no per-child cost.
- ElevenLabs (already integrated) is the alternative if you prefer its voice.

**Speaking by children:**
- **Off by default.** It stays off for every real child until the **legal review** and **written parent consent** are done (`REAL_LEARNER_PILOT_PLAN.md`).
- **Teachers** can turn it off per class; **parents** per child, in the parent app. The off setting always wins.
- When speaking is on: the audio goes to speech-to-text, is **not stored**, and only the transcript and score codes are kept.

**Tests:** unit tests; E2E: audio plays (asset loads, duration > 0), pictures load, a tap answer is recorded, and the speaking button is hidden when turned off. This is not tested by simulation; it is added to the pilot plan.

## 2. `mixedReview` (builds on `spacedReview` + `reviewFirst`)
- **Review items mix points:** when a review is due, the item mixes its point with a confusable or current one (for example a/an with the, or regular with irregular past), so the learner must choose the rule.
- **Source:** the rule bank's confusable pairs (the "interleaving" note in `engineV2.ts`).
- **One closing item** per session is mixed in the same way.
- **Tests:** a due review produces a mixed item line; with nothing due, nothing changes; the switch off means no change.

## 3. `realChat`
- **What it does:** 2–3 turns per session (after the first exercise block) are a real conversation, then back to the lesson.
- **Topics:**
  - **Under 13:** fixed safe lists only (animals, food, colours, toys and games, sport, school, weather and seasons).
  - **Teens and adults:** `learnerProfile` interests, with no personal-data questions (no address, phone, photos, or names of other people).
- **Errors:** corrected inside the talk by a recast, at most one per turn, the lesson's target form first.
- **Tests:** chat-turn selection, the topic list by age, the return to the lesson, and that the one-task rule still holds.

## 4. `adaptiveSpacing`
- **Next review date:**
  - an unassisted, first-try right answer skips one step (1 → 7 days, 3 → 21 days);
  - a right answer after a hint or a second try keeps the step;
  - a wrong answer resets the step to 1 day.
- **Unchanged:** an error closes only after an unassisted right answer at least 2 days after the last wrong one; the 21-day final check stays.
- **"Slow"** is measured by tries and hints. Response time is not available in text.
- **Tests:** each path, and the close rule kept.

## ESL7c (simulation; runs without waiting once 2–4 are on)
**Arms:**
- **NRF:** today's best, including `childRule`;
- **NRF+3:** NRF + `mixedReview` + `realChat` + `adaptiveSpacing`;
- **P+.**

**Design:** the same outcomes, skills, age bands, calendar and tests as ESL7. Pre-registered before any reply.

**Sample size:**
- From ESL7/7b, the spread of a learner's score difference between two arms was 12.6 points.
- To detect **3 points** with 80% power (two-sided, α = 0.05) needs **139 learners** per comparison, or 186 for 90% power.
- I'll use **144 learners**, so each outcome is taught to 8 learners per arm.
- The primary comparison is NRF+3 − NRF. NRF − P+ is reported but is under-powered (it needs about 240).

**Cost:** about 560 agent runs, roughly 2× ESL7, about 1.5–2 days.
- Learners are Sonnet tutors; judges and scorers are Opus.
- It runs in two cohorts of 72 and resumes after usage-limit stops.
- If usage runs out, I'll report the number reached and analyse the largest finished cohort.

**Limit:** with only 4 sessions in 2 weeks, `adaptiveSpacing` can change few review dates, so its effect may be small here.

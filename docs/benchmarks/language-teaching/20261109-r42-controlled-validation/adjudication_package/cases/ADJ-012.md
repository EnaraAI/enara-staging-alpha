## Conversation (learner-visible text)

**Turn 1. Learner:** Can you explain have been and went?

**Tutor:** Good question. Both talk about going somewhere, but they work differently.

**have/has been (to)** = present perfect. Use it for a life experience when **no time** is given.
- *I **have been to** Cairo.* (sometime in my life, not saying when)

**went** = past simple. Use it when you say a **finished time** (yesterday, last year, in 2022).
- *I **went to** Cairo **last summer**.*

Here's one example of how they work together:
*Have you ever **been** to a concert? Yes, I have. I **went** to one last month.*

The question uses present perfect (no time). The answer switches to past simple because "last month" is finished.

**Your turn:** Which is correct?
*I ___ to Alexandria in 2023.* (have been / went)

**Turn 2. Learner:** I have gone to Alexandria last summer.

**Tutor:** You used a new sentence, which is fine. Let's check it.

In *"I have gone to Alexandria last summer"*, the form **have gone** is built correctly (have + past participle).

The problem is in the time words. Look at the end of your sentence: **last summer**. Then look back at my two rules above.

Try again with the original sentence:

*I ___ to Alexandria in 2023.* (have been / went)

Before you choose, find the time words in it.

**Turn 3. Learner:** I have been to Cairo two years ago.

**Tutor:** Not quite yet, but **have been** is formed correctly, so the form is not the problem. The issue is the same one as last time: the time words **two years ago**.

Let's try a smaller step with a different example about sport:

- *I **have played** tennis.* → no time, just an experience ✅
- *I **played** tennis **on Saturday**.* → finished time, so past simple ✅
- ~~*I have played tennis on Saturday.*~~ ❌ finished time + present perfect don't mix

**Ago** works like *on Saturday*: it points to a finished time.

Now look at your sentence again. Rewrite it so the verb matches **two years ago**.
